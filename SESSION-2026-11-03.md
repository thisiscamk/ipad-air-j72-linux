# 3 November: combined input, storage and power session

## Results observed so far

One direct-Pi DFU booted the unchanged #35 RAM payload. The separate
[touch recovery-window candidate](code/touch/recovery-window35/) passed the
existing start/stop self-check, firmware health and physical restoration checks.
The original module file and cold-boot image were unchanged. The owner confirmed
menus, Foot typing and keyboard hide/tap/reopen on the RAM desktop.

Wi-Fi association, HTTPS and pinned-key Wi-Fi SSH pass. Wi-Fi reports power saving
already enabled; this session did not change that setting. Wi-Fi control survived
the move to the same qualified powered JCH377 charging/data port. Rebinding the
same USB device controller restored USB communication without another DFU.

The same-boot storage inventory passed its firmware/DRAM/reservation checks. The
opt-in ASP driver exposed the 128 GB drive read-only. Six bounded 4 KiB reads
matched in repeated pairs, including both GPT signatures. No native filesystem
was mounted, and no internal write, resize or installation occurred.

## Completed measurement

The initial battery reading was 100%, 4.121 V and 32.0°C, above the existing
charging entry criteria. Charging was not enabled at that point.

After the owner checked the normal 1604/2047 brightness, the measurement was
fixed at 802/2047 with the existing `schedutil` governor. The five-minute idle
capture with ASP absent completed with 28 readings; reported current ranged
from −658 to −484 mA and temperature from 32.1 to 32.8°C. Gauge readings can be
cached. These values are an initial discharge baseline, not a charging result
or proof of a causal power saving.

The matching ASP-present capture also completed with 28 readings. Over the
recorded sample coverage, the left-held time-weighted means were −606.917 mA
(ASP absent, 295.224 seconds) and −596.188 mA (ASP present, 296.361 seconds).
Temperature and battery state drifted between captures; these overlapping
results do not isolate an ASP power cost or prove a saving. No power policy
was changed between recordings.

Charging began at 07:10 local time, after about 19 minutes waiting for the
existing entry criteria: 90%, 4.035 V and 34.5°C. The 5400.657-second run
completed with Wi-Fi, touch and read-only ASP active at fixed brightness 802.

## Input passes the bounded run; power loses net charge

The touch worker retained **221,923 valid frames**, eight recoveries, zero SPI
errors, zero layout failures and zero timeouts. Normal stop, firmware health
and physical restoration passed. This passes the former lifetime recovery limit
without claiming all-day stability or an end-of-run owner gesture check.
There were 56,595 wire anomalies; the retained bounded ring still provides a
protocol-resynchronization lead. Twenty of 32 retained data-phase records are
checksum-valid 16-byte E1 headers. Raw records remain private and no resync
change has been applied. [Sanitized input result](code/touch/recovery-window35/DEVICE-RESULT-20261103.json).

The power run collected **2553 fresh vectors**, covering 5397.611 seconds with
a maximum 2.237-second sample gap. Time-weighted current was **−120.81 mA**:
**−181.13 mAh** including every hold. The gauge moved 90% →85%; peak temperature
was 36.7°C. This is a **net-charge failure**, despite correct restoration.
[Sanitized power result](code/charging/maintain35/NINETY-MINUTE-RESULT.json).

All five active pauses began at 4.201 V. Four automatic voltage-hold restarts
passed. After the fifth, temperature stayed above the 36.5°C restart gate;
the controller entered WAIT_COOL at elapsed 4150.823 seconds and remained off.
There was no actual reading above the 38°C thermal cutoff. This is not a
thermal-trip/cooldown/restart pass, and differs from the 2 November thermal hold.

The controller ended disabled without a fault; independent readback verified
input-limit baseline `02`. Normal desktop/input/radio cleanup passed and a
normal iOS reboot was requested. The iPad did not reappear over USB, so **iOS
screen state and charging remain unverified**. A follow-up helper incorrectly
treated an absent port as Linux; its corrected host-tested version now waits
for re-enumeration and records absence explicitly, without a recovery attempt.
